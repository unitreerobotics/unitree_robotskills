# Reading the board from R1-D head images (boardview / fencheck)

English | [中文](board_view.zh.md)

## When to use
- Before writing the FEN in `board.json` each round, or when verifying the opponent's reply.
- Especially when the far Black half is crowded together in the raw image and cannot be seen clearly.
- When you suspect that a piece in the FEN is written wrongly or missing.

## Not to be used for
**These images are only for reading the position.** They are resampled pictures and **cannot be used to estimate the XYZ/RPY of source/target**, nor can they replace the planning image as the basis for poses. Poses are still determined by the raw planning image, `round build`, and preflight.

## Prerequisites
- `config/head_camera_calibration.json` exists (the eye-on-head calibration result, see `setup/head-camera-calibration`).
- `config/board_geometry.json` matches the current board position. When the board has been moved, first measure again with `scripts/head_board_measure.py --prior config/board_geometry.json`.
- The planning image was taken with the head in the locked pose, and the head angles at that time are known. A planning image taken with `client capture_cameras` carries both automatically.

## Steps
Run in the package root directory.

1. **Generate the reference images** (using this round's planning image response):
   ```bash
   bash r1d boardview --observation <run>/tmp/<capture response>.json --output <run>/images/board_view
   ```
   - You can also give the raw image directly: `--image <*_stereo.jpg>` (it can be repeated for multiple frames, which are averaged to reduce noise) plus `--head-q <pitch> <yaw>`.
   - The head yaw backlash is about ±15 mrad. When the approach direction is not the negative direction, add `--yaw-direction 1`.
2. **Check the alignment first.** Open `board_topdown.jpg`; the green crosses should fall on the intersections of the board lines.
   - If the whole grid is off by one square, or clearly misaligned, the board has been moved: stop reading the board and first re-measure the board geometry.
3. **Read the position.** Read in FEN order: from rank 9 (far side, Black, top of the image) to rank 0 (near side, Red, bottom of the image), and each rank from a to i (a is on the robot's left-hand side, the left of the image).
   - Compare the characters with the legend at the bottom: red characters are uppercase, black characters are lowercase. Chariot 车 r/R, horse 马 n/N, elephant 象/相 b/B, advisor 士/仕 a/A, general 将/帥 k/K, cannon 炮 c/C, soldier 卒/兵 p/P.
   - The cannon (炮) of Black and Red is the same character and can only be told apart by the ink colour. The elephant (象/相), advisor (士/仕), general (将/帥) and soldier (卒/兵) use different characters on the two sides.
   - When unsure, look at `board_tiles.jpg`: **a square counts as occupied only when the centre of the character falls inside the green circle of that square**. Far pieces show their sides, and the edge of another piece may be visible in an adjacent square.
   - When the far characters are blurry, judge them together with `undistorted_left.jpg` and the raw planning image.
4. **Verify the FEN:**
   ```bash
   bash r1d fencheck "<FEN> w - - 0 1" --view <run>/images/board_view
   ```
   - It outputs a text board and the piece counts of both sides, and checks the format, the numbers of ranks and files, the upper limit of each piece type, and whether the generals are inside the palace.
   - Open the generated `fen_overlay.jpg`: each intersection shows the letter from your FEN, a red frame is Red, a black frame is Black, and a green dot means an empty square. **Compare the letter with the piece underneath square by square**; any mismatch means the FEN must be corrected and verified again.
   - `ok: true` only means the FEN format is legal, not that it matches the board; consistency can only be established by looking at the overlay square by square.
5. **Write `board.json`**: put the raw planning image path in `evidence`; `fen_overlay.jpg` can be attached as the board-reading basis. Then check move legality with `bash r1d engine validate/analyze` as usual.

## Outputs (all under the `--output` directory)
| File | Content |
|---|---|
| `board_topdown.jpg` | Top-down view projected onto the plane of the piece tops, Red at the bottom, rank and file coordinates marked around it, with a piece legend |
| `board_tiles.jpg` | The 90 intersections enlarged square by square, each with a circle of piece size |
| `undistorted_left.jpg`, `undistorted_right.jpg` | Ordinary perspective views with the fisheye curvature removed |
| `board_view.json` | Input parameters, and the pixel position of each intersection in the top-down view |
| `fen_overlay.jpg` | Generated by `fencheck --view` |

## Limitations
- **The improvement in far-side sharpness is limited.** The far-side resolution is limited by the camera, about 11–17 px per square. The rectification only makes the geometry and layout clearer and does not add detail.
- **The board lines show slight ghosting.** The top-down view is aligned to the piece tops, the board lines are one piece height below this plane, and the parallax between the two eyes makes the lines ghost; this does not affect reading the characters.
- **The board must match the geometry configuration.** Any move makes the grid misaligned, so the alignment check in step 2 cannot be skipped.
