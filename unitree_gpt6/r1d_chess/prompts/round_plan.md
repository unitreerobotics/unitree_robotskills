# R1-D per-round chess prompt

English | [中文](round_plan.zh.md)

First read [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) and the [operation manual](../docs/operation_manual.md). Run artifacts go in `runs/<this-round>/`, and the evaluation templates are in `assets/templates/`.

If this round is planning only, stop after planning and preflight; enable and execute only when this round has been authorised to actually move pieces. A `model_estimate` profile with complete values and a clear basis is sufficient for planning, preflight, and execution, without waiting for a physical calibration; record the source truthfully, and do not mark an estimate as `calibration_verified=true`. When TCP, init, scene, or release height is missing, do read-only observation only.

## Model, profile, and tracking settings (required reading)

- **Kinematic and gravity model**: `assets/models/r1d/r1d.urdf` in the package (includes Dex1, wrist pitch limit ±0.5236), specified by `model_urdf` in `config/runtime.json`. Do not use any other URDF to compute TCP, init, or the scene, and do not set `R1D_MODEL_URDF` to override it.
- **The profile can only be the current `config/robot.json` or derived from it**: if the board has not moved, use it directly; if the board or the scene changes, copy it, change `scene`, `board_release_z_m`, `tray_release_z_m`, and `estimate_evidence`, and then load it with `serve --profile`. You must keep `kinematic_model_sha256`, `init_xyz_rpy`, and `arm_tracking_limit_rad` (0.20). When `kinematic_model_sha256` is missing or does not match the current URDF, both service start and preflight refuse; do not copy a hash by hand to make it pass the check, and the geometry must be estimated with the current model.
- **Torque feedforward is on by default**: `config/arm_torque_ff.json` is loaded automatically by the service; do not modify or disable it unless the user explicitly asks. The `arm_torque_ff` returned by `get_state` can be used to verify that it is in effect.
- **Tracking limits**: during operation, a joint tracking error above 0.20 rad latches a fault; the execution start point requires joint deviation ≤0.08 rad and TCP deviation ≤15 mm. After a fault, handle it according to the actual state; do not relax thresholds in order to pass a check.
- **Scene parameters** are in `config/robot.json` (TCP at the gripper flange, `scene`, release heights), and the board intersections are in `config/board_geometry.json`; for their meaning see `docs/r1d_scene_parameters.md`. After the board moves, measure again first (`bash r1d boardcal`; for a large move, first do a coarse measurement with `python scripts/head_board_measure.py --prior config/board_geometry.json`; the prior prevents the grid from being off by one square).
- **The head is locked at `head_q_rad` in `config/robot.json`** (close to the lowest head position). When the service takes over, it ramps the head to that position, and all planning images are taken from this viewpoint.
- **Pick and place pose: pitch 0.8 rad, using the front part of the gripper (the grip point is 137 mm from the flange).** `bash r1d pose` uses this pose by default: for the grasp the fingertips are 3 mm above the board surface; **for all moves the board release TCP z is fixed at 0.150 m, independent of pitch** (the tray follows the profile); the release gripper opening is 3.0 when the neighbouring points on both sides in the opening direction are empty, otherwise 2.3 (`round build` decides automatically); use 0.175 for `gate_z_m` (0.16 does not satisfy the transport clearance).
- **The source/target/capture poses in the spec are the TCP (Dex1 flange), not the piece position.** Grip point = TCP + 0.137·(cos p·cos y, cos p·sin y, −sin p). Always generate the pose block with `bash r1d pose pose --role source|target|tray --square <square> [--fingers=-y|+y|+x] [--offset-xy dx dy]`, then fill in this round's image basis. `round build` verifies that the grip point is within 12 mm of the intersection of the stated square, and a TCP placed directly on the intersection is rejected; `bash r1d pose check --spec round_spec.json` can do the check on its own.
- **Preflight** uses analytic-Jacobian damped least-squares IK; a full move preflight usually takes tens of seconds to two minutes. Clearly more than a few minutes is abnormal; check the service log first, and do not resend or rebuild the plan.
- **Preflight is based on the live position and checks only the acting arm**: `prepare_transaction` reads the live joints when it starts, the waypoints of the non-acting arm are replaced by its live pose (hold only), and the first lift waypoint of the acting arm uses the live x/y/rpy instead. If the arm sinks a few millimetres after the image is taken, there is no need to retake it. Contacts by the non-acting arm are not intercepted.

## Steps of each round

0. **Fault or return to position**: when the arm stops because of an error during execution, or the user asks to return to position, run `bash r1d retract` directly (it replays the executed path in reverse order without preflight; it closes the gripper while returning to the transport clearance height). `retract` cannot replace gate approval or the success path; do not open the gripper manually with `client set_gripper` to bypass the transaction. When ending the task, press Ctrl-C to stop the service; it unloads the arms automatically (the arms will drop).
0. **Setup check**: before the first move on this machine, run `python scripts/setup_status.py --require`. While any required item is still a reference value, do not enable motion; first complete it following `setup/README.md` (local environment, camera check, arm accuracy calibration, head camera calibration, chess scene parameters, board calibration), or have the user explicitly decide to keep it and record the basis.
0. **After a robot restart, before taking over**: when the lift, head, waist, both arms, or the grippers are not at the initial pose, first run `bash r1d goinit` to preview, hand it to the user to confirm it is safe, and only then run `bash r1d goinit --execute` (it does no preflight at all; do not execute it on your own); after `ready_for_chess=true` appears in `--status`, start the service and take over with `handover --execute`. To return to init during play, use `prepare_init`/`execute_init`.
0. **At the start of the task, first start the chess service and take over the robot** ("Starting a chess task" in the operation manual):
   - `systemctl --user start r1d-runtime.service` starts the Runtime (the robot runtime, `third_party/robot_runtime`; the unit is generated by `python scripts/install_runtime_service.py --write`), `bash r1d serve --allow-motion --arm <arm>` stays resident in the background, then run `handover --execute` (when a hold unit exists) or `client enable_control`.
   - After that, communicate with the robot only through this service: init, moves, and captures all use `client prepare_*`/`execute_*`, checkpoints use `serial next` and `gate`, and you do not call the Runtime or other motion scripts directly.
   - After `get_state` confirms that the head has reached the reference position, run `client release_control` to disable execution (the session is kept: `control_enabled=false`, `head_waist_session_active=true`), then take the planning image and plan. `round build`, `set_active_arm`, and `prepare_init` all require execution to be disabled.
   - **After the move finishes and returns to init (or after `retract`), the service unloads the arms automatically and ends the session, so you must take over again at the start of every round**: when the entry conditions are met run `client enable_control`, otherwise first run `goinit` (the user must confirm it is safe) and then `handover --execute`; then `release_control`, then plan.
1. Verify the Runtime, the external hold unit, that the feedback is fresh and stationary, that the grippers are empty, the head and waist reference positions (at takeover the head may be off by 0.35 rad and is ramped over), and the lift height recorded in the profile. When the TCP of each arm is ≤10 cm from its own init, do not initialise again, and start from the joints and XYZ/RPY measured this round; **look only at the TCP distance; a joint configuration different from init is not a problem**. When it is more than 10 cm after unloading, first run `prepare_init`/`execute_init`. The non-acting arm keeps its current position.
2. In a new run, capture only the head single-eye `cam_left_high` planning image, and confirm the complete position and the opponent's actual reply; write the FEN, the actual move history, and the image evidence. Match against this game's prediction, and search again on a mismatch or when the prediction is exhausted. Prefer the fastest reliable winning line, and do not assume the opponent follows the principal variation. When the far Black half cannot be seen clearly, follow `board_position/prompts/board_view.md` and use `bash r1d boardview` to generate the top-down view and the per-square enlarged image to write the FEN, then use `bash r1d fencheck` to draw it back onto the image and check square by square; these images are only for reading the board and cannot be used to estimate source/target poses.
3. Choose the arm: prefer the left arm when the source is in files a–d and the right arm when it is in files e–i; reaching across the whole board is allowed. Independently estimate the TCP XYZ/RPY of source/target for that arm this round; for each pose prefer the lateral orientation, and try the longitudinal one when there is interference or it is unreachable. Decide the candidates by the reachability from the full-branch preflight: when the preferred arm fails, re-estimate, build, and preflight for the other arm, and change the move only when both arms fail. For a capture, first put the opponent's piece into the tray on the same side, then move your own piece, with the whole sequence preflighted jointly; when the target orientation differs from the source, build arranges a turn in place above the tray.
4. Before execution (execution state disabled, taken over again this round), have ready the service endpoint, Python, run/request/prepared/execute/result, and the checkpoint paths of each stage. After localisation, chess rules, poses, and the full-branch preflight are prepared: first run `get_state` to confirm `state_age_s` is normal and wait about 3 s, then run `client enable_control`, verify `control_enabled=true` and no fault, and then submit execute only once. Wait for execute without a time limit, and send no other request before the client returns (no separate `get_state`/`capture`/hold). Execution is disabled automatically when it ends.
5. **You judge the gates and submit immediately; do not ask the user gate by gate.** Always use `bash r1d gate --prepared ... --checkpoint ... --decision ... --reason "..." [--offset dx dy]`; send every gate command and every `serial next` on its own, with absolute paths.
   - No gate (source high, pre-close, recapture after correction, pre-release) has a time limit; the service holds the current position until it receives a decision. No time limit does not mean you can be slow: the target for each gate is about 5 s, and after `next` returns `needs_review` do only "look at the two images → submit → immediately `next`", without reading code, changing code, checking logs, sending `get_state`/`capture`, or running tests in between. Record the real time taken truthfully.
   - Each time, look only at this checkpoint's head image and the acting arm's wrist image. All adjustments during execution (XY correction, pitch reduction, return to the high position, release correction) only solve IK and send the result directly, without preflight.
   - Source high gate: `success` if the piece is midway between the two jaws, otherwise `adjust` (at most three times).
   - Pre-close gate: when the piece centre has passed the jaw tips and is roughly between the two jaws, submit `success` to close the gripper, and do not return to the high position because of a lateral offset of a few millimetres; when most of the piece is beyond the fingertips use `adjust_pitch` (reduce the downward pitch by 0.1 rad, TCP position unchanged, recapture, at most two times); use `retry_high` only if the piece is so clearly off that a single jaw would push it away. Do not use `adjust_z` (disabled by default). When an empty grasp is detected after closing, the service automatically rises back to the high position, recaptures, and grasps again, with at most three real closes.
6. After a successful grasp, transport continuously, and recapture after descending at the target and before opening the gripper. **At the release gate you must verify the landing point independently with the wrist image and cannot rely on FK alone** (FK and the board geometry come from the same source, so FK cannot show an error in the geometry): along the finger direction find a known neighbouring piece or a printed intersection, use the piece diameter as the local scale, measure the distance from the centre of the gripped piece to it, and compare with the expected square spacing; do the same laterally with the grid lines on both sides. When the deviation in either direction is >5 mm, submit `adjust` (give the deviation in the base frame, in metres), keeping Z/RPY and doing a low-position XY correction; at most three times, with a recapture after the first two. For the last `adjust` you must confirm from the current two images that the predicted landing point after the correction can be released safely (`release_alignment_verified=true`, with the basis stated in reason), otherwise both the client and the server refuse; using up the quota cannot replace the confirmation. When you cannot judge, there is a collision risk, or the user aborts, do not let it pass: before release, stop with `failure` and keep holding the piece; for an explicit abort before the grasp, retract with `serial submit --failure`.
7. After `success` is submitted at the release gate, the service completes the following automatically and continuously: open the gripper in place → hold 0.5 s → retreat vertically upward to clearance with the gripper open → retreat to staging with the gripper open → close the gripper in place → return to init at the high position (the final joints equal `init_joint_rad`). There are no gates in between, and no further command is needed; review after `next` reads `finished`. The two segments of a capture follow each other continuously. During execution and while holding a piece, do not look up material, change code, run tests, run engine searches, or produce FK reports.
8. After the return to init, the service unloads the arms automatically (`arm_unload` in the result), and the next round requires taking over again. After completion do only two things: judge success or misplacement from a new head image; compute offline the three kinds of FK error for the two events, successful grasp and low position before release, then merge them into `docs/第N轮_中文着法_北京时间.json` (round N, Chinese move notation, Beijing time) and the error JSONL. Present the image conclusion and the FK error side by side, and mark missing data truthfully. Real-robot results, simulation results, and accurate landing on the square must not be confused.

After the review, report only the success or misplacement conclusion, the FK error, the file paths, and whether unloading succeeded. Restoring AI or shutting down the service requires a separate explicit request and verification of the actual state.

## Reference

The following comes from measurements on one reference robot and only describes situations you may encounter; confirm them yourself on a new robot:

- At the low position the arm is actually about 1 cm higher than commanded, the pre-close gate often shows most of the piece beyond the fingertips, and this is handled with `adjust_pitch`.
- When the release gate was passed on FK alone, landing deviations of about 2 cm occurred.
- The left arm can reach the left tray only with finger yaw −0.8 (`pose --role tray --tray left --yaw=-0.8`).
- Piece diameter 29.7 mm, adjacent files 35.6 mm, adjacent ranks 34.2 mm, edge gap between adjacent pieces about 6 mm.
- During the retry rise after an empty grasp, the arm tracking lags, and the service waits for the arm to arrive (at most 2 s) before judging whether the transport clearance has been reached.
